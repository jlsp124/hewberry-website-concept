## Selected direction

The valley after dark combines the calm of a lit property with the precision of an alarm signal. The concept carries forward Hewberry's published shield and wordmark, Work Sans, charcoal and amber. The original signal motif reappears in a simple monitoring diagram. The public header identifies the site as an independent concept.

The home opening reads in three beats. First, a dusk property sets the scene beside one sentence explaining installation and monitoring. Second, a service index helps homeowners, operators and builders recognize their project. Third, a plain-language monitoring sequence leads into Vernon-based support and the guided quote. The same service names carry from the index to detail routes and into preselected quote paths.

The image entrance uses a short settling movement with no text reveal gate. As desktop users scroll voluntarily, the architecture recedes slightly. The monitoring line progresses once when it enters view, with three readable steps in the document. Native disclosure and link affordances serve touch, pointer and keyboard. Reduced motion skips the entrance, scroll effect and signal animation.

## Cinematic study

A real-time architectural model could depict time of day and a moving camera. A convincing version would require original architectural modelling, careful material and lighting work, compression, and a separate mobile rendering strategy. Commercially reusable material and HDRI sources exist. Model rights, art quality and property attribution would still need individual review. The best use of the concept budget is one detailed photographic moment and a separate abstract explanation of what a monitored signal does.

The opening uses real, credited photography, responsive WebP and ordinary HTML over a deep neutral fallback. The property photos are editorial examples. The landscape depicts a real local place identified in its caption. Movement is ornamental and the signal story stays conceptual. The researched Canadian crime statistic is documented in [research](research.md). The opening focuses on the customer's decision.

## Design and delivery checks

- Verify recognition by swapping the copy with another service business. Vernon, installed systems, the service vocabulary, and the monitoring sequence should make the combination specific to Hewberry.
- Keep the equivalent keyboard and touch path visible. Preserve the answer when a decorative image cannot load. Keep all messaging readable at the reduced-motion setting.
- Ask for property type and areas of interest, with a path for people who are unsure. Make the simulated submit explicit and retain choices when the visitor goes back.
- Use a separate static page for each useful entry point and a distinctive mobile crop for first view. Document human and browser-based verification in [QA](qa.md).

## Typography and composition revision

The first pass shared too much visual grammar with Recplace. The revised opening uses one display line at wide widths, a compact row of service copy and actions, and architectural photography in its own wide frame. Bricolage Grotesque gives the display type a more characterful silhouette. Work Sans carries the existing brand connection into navigation and longer text.

DBYDT informed the change in scale between display type and utility text. BCG informed how a single architectural subject can hold the frame, with more breathing room around the copy and controls at narrower widths. Eleos informed putting the identity in the opening composition and treating photography as a full visual beat. Recplace’s split opening and repeating two-column editorial rhythm were the contrast case. [DBYDT](https://www.dbydt.space/), [BCG Building Design](https://www.bcgbuildingdesign.com.au/), [Eleos](https://www.eleos.la/), [Recplace](https://recplace.ca/).

The amber signal point first appears beside the Vernon location, then reappears in the monitoring sequence. The services section has one image and an adjacent selector. The actual Kalamalka Lake photograph gets a wider frame with its own caption. Content and actions are present on first paint. Motion remains attached to photography and the explanation of a signal.

The walkthrough applies Getty’s idea of teaching through a sequence of objects, ERA Residence’s pacing between scenes, and Oryzo’s habit of describing a benefit before naming the equipment behind it. Desktop keeps one photographic frame in view while the explanation progresses. Small screens and reduced-motion readers get five ordinary, illustrated chapters. The ending offers a quote and the Vernon phone number. [Getty’s Persepolis Reimagined](https://persepolis.getty.edu/), [ERA Residence](https://www.era-residence.com/), [Oryzo](https://oryzo.ai/).

## Dusk-to-night concept

The photographed equipment sequence made visitors infer how unrelated objects fit together. The new opening keeps one real property in view. Its lighting changes as the reader moves from an ordinary evening to a door opening and an operator receiving an alert. The house and a single amber signal connect those moments. Pointer depth and a playable, clearly labelled signal example give the visitor an immediate response alongside the written explanation.

Hewberry’s own claim of more than 25 years of industry experience leads the page. Two header links take visitors to the installation table or contact. The customer quote comes from Hewberry’s published testimonial. The close offers the Vernon office phone number and the simulated quote. The visual reference is Oryzo’s habit of returning to one recognizable subject and showing its use before listing specs. [Hewberry Alarms](https://www.hewberryalarms.ca/), [Lusion’s Oryzo case study](https://lusion.co/projects/oryzo_ai/).

## Product story revision

| Direction | What it shows | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doorway at dusk | A photographed entrance leads into a close-up of the door opening, an Ajax hub receiving the signal and a monitoring operator receiving the alert. Technical details follow in a visual product index. | Puts the installation and its purpose in one story. The opening photo and demonstration need distinct, credible compositions at every width. |
| Product studio | A bright tabletop presents a row of Ajax equipment. The viewer examines each item and compares it in a catalogue. | Strong for hardware recognition. The story could feel like a manufacturer catalogue and give too little space to Hewberry's service. |
| Property cutaway | An architectural model reveals several protection layers around the building. | Shows broad scope quickly. A whole building divides attention and requires more visual production to feel believable. |

The doorway at dusk is the chosen direction. A real entrance, painted timber, pearl-white hardware and a copper signal give the page a common visual vocabulary. Inter Tight provides clear utility and confident display scale. Instrument Serif adds a warmer note to the opening and customer testimony. Hewberry's published shield, years of experience, and customer quote carry the identity.

The opening introduces the company in one sentence. The walkthrough shows a door moving, the system sending an alert, and a person receiving it. Each action gets its own composition and caption. The equipment index and installation services make the next decision easy, followed by the Vernon office and simulated quote.

Oryzo informed the photographed setting, dedicated close-ups and continuity between shots. HALO/02 and VYRA show ways to make the featured object visibly change with scroll. The labelling and 3D geometry here are original to this concept. The models are Ajax examples for the demo. Hewberry can specify its current equipment when it reviews the site. [Lusion on Oryzo's 3D design](https://blog.lusion.co/oryzo-bts-part-2-7-3d-design-and-motion-graphics) · [HALO/02](https://github.com/NachoTorresRD/interactive-product-showcase) · [VYRA](https://github.com/arslanagayev/vyra-energy)

The 3D scene uses native scroll. The camera angle and door opening follow it continuously. Pointer input adjusts the view and lighting slightly so the object remains the focus. Touch gets its own closer framing. The complete captions and illustrated states remain available with reduced motion, script failures and unsupported graphics.

## Editorial refinement

The public browser builds of all four references were captured locally. The study used the shipped HTML, CSS and JavaScript, scroll and pointer recordings, and computed type measurements at desktop and phone widths.

| Reference | Observed sequence and treatment | Hewberry application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Oryzo](https://oryzo.ai/) | A real tabletop opens the story. The object moves into a claim, use, a technical figure, proof and a spec sheet. The numerical detail appears with its drawing. Individual scenes alternate dark material close-ups with spare captions. | Put the verified local figure in its own scene, then give each object one job. Reveal the framed 3D shot over its corresponding still photograph. |
| [Lusion](https://lusion.co/) | The studio describes what it does above a large framed work sample. Later type and images demonstrate that service in different proportions. Cursor responses stay with the scene and preview. | Introduce Hewberry as the installer, demonstrate the system in its own frames, then reveal installation options through imagery and large service titles. |
| [Lusion Labs](https://labs.lusion.co/) | Image previews carry the collection. Set titles, restrained labels and hover states make the body of work easy to skim. | Show a single large installation preview on desktop and link directly to the relevant service. Group Ajax equipment on the technical page. |
| [Atlas Motion](https://atlasmotion.com/) | One physical product family introduces the premise. The material story leads into a short sequence of integration, evaluation and manufacturing. | Keep the alarm demonstration to a door, hub and monitoring response. Place a direct installation explanation after the demonstration, followed by contact. |

The palette uses warm ivory, charcoal and copper. Headings, captions and gutters use the viewport to retain readable scale on a wide or zoomed-out window. The story canvas is attached to the measured bounds of one shot at a time. The surrounding text and the photo fallback retain normal document layout. The viewport-to-document positioning is adapted from [Lusion’s MIT WebGL Scroll Sync](https://github.com/lusionltd/WebGL-Scroll-Sync). Local pointer clamping is adapted from [ObsidianUI’s MIT hover image](https://www.obsidianui.dev/r/hover-img.json), found in the design library.
